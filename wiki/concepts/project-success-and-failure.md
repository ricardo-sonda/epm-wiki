# Project success and failure

Success means meeting (internal and external) expectations — classically **on time, on budget, to specification** — but each stakeholder judges it differently, which forces trade-offs ([[gpe-slides-1-2026-2027]]).

## Perspectives

- **PM:** deadlines, budgets, initial objectives.
- **Customer:** specific benefits delivered smoothly into operations.
- **Team:** enjoyment, learning, career development.

## Four success dimensions (Shenhar et al. 2001)

1. **Impact on the customer** — meets specs and needs, actually used, satisfaction.
2. **Project efficiency** — schedule and budget goals.
3. **Business success for the performing organization** — commercial success, market share.
4. **Future potential** — new market/product line, new technology.

On time and on budget alone = efficiency only (PC1 quiz item 9, [[pc1-gpe-2026-2027]]).

## Measured success rates

- Standish CHAOS (IT): ~29–31% successful, ~50% challenged, ~17–22% failed (2011–2020; 2020 was the last edition).
- PMI *Maximizing Project Success* (2024): judging by value delivered, 47% of IT projects succeed (41% mixed, 12% failed). The yardstick changes the answer; methods differ.
- By industry, success ranges from Industrials (net score 47) to Consulting (11); by funding, bank/for-profit investors fare best and international/foreign government worst.

## Why projects fail

Failed projects average +98.5% cost, +115% duration and only 61% of scope; >50% of problems happened before and were not documented. Top causes: lack of user input (12.8%), incomplete requirements (12.3%), changing requirements (11.8%), lack of executive support (7.5%), technology incompetence (7%), lack of resources (6.4%), unrealistic expectations (5.9%), unclear objectives (5.3%). The slides summarise: **communication and requirements**. Other causes: poor resource planning, unrealistic objectives/schedules/budgets, undefined CSFs, no risk planning, uncontrolled scope ([[change-control-and-scope-creep]]).

## Non-classic view

Projects are tools for executing strategy, so success should also reflect alignment with organizational goals (Ulusoy & Hazır 2021); a remote-care example uses 11 criteria with costs assessed separately.

## Exercise lessons

Customer satisfaction or market success alone is insufficient; XPTO's unused app shows requirement/communication failure; the architect's villa shows the customer must be involved throughout, not only at approval ([[gpe-questions-and-exercises]] ex. 2).
