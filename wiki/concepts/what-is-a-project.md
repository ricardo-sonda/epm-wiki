# What is a project

A project is a temporary, unique, resource-consuming endeavour with a specific objective, performed by a temporary team, to be achieved at a given time and budget ([[gpe-slides-1-2026-2027]]).

## Standard definitions

- **PMI (PMBOK 7th, 2021):** "A temporary endeavor undertaken to create a unique product, service or result."
- **IPMA (ICB 3.0):** "A time- and cost-constrained operation to realize a set of defined deliverables … up to quality standards and requirements."
- **ISO 21500 (2012):** "A unique set of processes consisting of coordinated and controlled activities with start and finish dates, undertaken to achieve an objective."

## Projects vs operations

| Projects ("transform the business") | Operations ("run the business") |
|---|---|
| Limited duration | Ongoing |
| Something must change at the customer | Repetitive tasks, efficiency focus |
| High uncertainty, demands creativity | Low uncertainty, standard procedures |
| Deliverables transferred to operations | "Quality in routine" |

Classroom classification: getting married, opening a business, building a bookcase, implementing a QMS → project; staying married, managing a fleet or payroll → operations; publishing a monthly journal depends on scope. PC1: "managing a store" is not a project ([[pc1-gpe-2026-2027]]).

## Why projects exist

Need → evaluation → strategy → projects → change: projects are **how companies implement their strategy**. Even operations-oriented organizations (Autoeuropa, beverage makers) need PM for new products, process changes and training; in a mixed company, design-to-order work is project-based while partner sales are operations-based ([[gpe-questions-and-exercises]] ex. 1).

## Organization types

Project-oriented organizations earn most income from projects (consulting, construction, agencies, film producers, law firms, training firms); operations-oriented / project-dependent ones earn it from stable production and sale (industry, commerce, 3PL, telecoms, public services).
