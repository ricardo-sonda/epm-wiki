# Project charter

The primary document defining the project and setting the general framework for its implementation (Haugan 2002, in [[gpe-slides-4-2026-2027]]). Output of the *Develop Project Charter* process in Initiating ([[project-life-cycle-and-process-groups]]).

## What it does

- Consolidates everything known so far; acts as a "contract" between team and stakeholders (a mutual agreement with an internal customer).
- With an **external customer it is usually not the commercial contract**, which is negotiated after an approved RFP response (possibly with a draft Project Execution Plan).
- Ensures a common understanding of outcome (intermediate/final deliverables, completion/success criteria) and business justification (triggering need, strategic alignment, objectives, expected benefits).
- Identifies customer priorities (ASAP? no cost variance? full spec compliance?) and **assumptions and constraints**, essential for risk planning.
- It is high-level, not a detailed plan; assumptions are not verified facts ([[pc2-gpe-2026-2027]]).

## Triggering business needs

Market demand, organizational need, customer request, technological advance, legal requirement, ecological impact, social need.

## Typical contents (Ulusoy & Hazır 2021)

1. General information (name, sponsor, key stakeholders, impact)
2. Purpose / business justification
3. Measurable objectives and success criteria (see [[smart-objectives]])
4. PM (authority, responsibility, reporting) and team
5. Description: scope, deliverables, start/end dates, milestones, budget, resources
6. Preliminary risk analysis
7. Constraints
8. Closure: who decides success/failure, evaluation criteria, delivery rules
9. Ratification (signatures)

Crossrail critique: weakest elements were unmeasurable objectives ("world-class reliability") and no early risk analysis.
