# Risk and uncertainty

Projects run under uncertainty: the PM has only partial knowledge of outcome and situation (Meredith & Mantel 2011, in [[gpe-slides-2-2026-2027]]).

## Definitions

- **Project risk (PMI 2017):** an uncertain event or condition that, if it occurs, has a positive or negative effect on one or more project objectives (risks can be opportunities).
- **Risk vs uncertainty (March & Simon 1958):** risk = probabilities can be predicted; uncertainty = they cannot be reliably estimated.
- A risk-management culture that integrates risk tools into practice improves success (Voetsch et al. 2004).

## Types of uncertainty (De Meyer et al. 2002)

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Variation | Random small deviations | Short machine breakdowns; deliveries occasionally hours late |
| Foreseen uncertainty | Identifiable, anticipatable events | Rain; an announced transport strike |
| Unforeseen uncertainty | Events not known in advance | Last-minute change/redesign requests |
| Chaos | Utter confusion; crisis management | Natural disasters; a flood destroying the site |

(Examples from the slides and [[pc2-gpe-2026-2027]].)

## Practice points

- Identify risks widely, then **prioritise by probability and impact**; plan detailed responses only for significant risks and accept/monitor minor ones ([[pc1-gpe-2026-2027]]).
- Charter assumptions are unverified beliefs, so each is a potential risk; the charter should include a preliminary risk analysis ([[gpe-slides-4-2026-2027]]). Missing early risk analysis was a weakness of Crossrail's strategy document.
- "No risk planning" is a listed cause of failure ([[project-success-and-failure]]).
