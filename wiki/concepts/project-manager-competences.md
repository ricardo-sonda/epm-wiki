# Project manager role and competences

The PM has three core roles: **lead a team**, **manage expectations and relationships**, **manage risk** ([[gpe-slides-2-2026-2027]]). The summary formula: PM knowledge (PMBOK) + EQ and relationship skills + technical domain knowledge.

## Skills (Lockyer & Gordon 2005)

Technical: technology, project economics, system design/maintenance, planning and control, finance, procurement. Human: HR management, personal communication, negotiation and conflict resolution.

## Tasks

Plan what and when; recruit and coordinate the team; analyse interfaces and constraints; set the budget; run the project, anticipating problems; implement tools and methods; control execution; deliver the final product. Breaking down work (WBS) and coordinating dependencies are the PM's job, not the assignees' ([[pc1-gpe-2026-2027]]).

## Leadership

- Manager (plan, organize, recruit, control) vs leader (vision, teamwork, motivation); good PMs are both.
- Leadership = getting things done through others while earning respect, trust, loyalty, cooperation and commitment (Verma & Wilderman 1994); requires IQ and **emotional intelligence** (recognize and manage one's own and others' emotions — Stein 2009). PC2 examples: private check-ins, calm with bad news, praise in public/criticise in private ([[pc2-gpe-2026-2027]]).
- Team development follows Tuckman's (1965) stages; good team members communicate, solve problems, share risk, stay committed, support others and cooperate outside the team. Select team members for interpersonal fit too, not only technical skill.

## Certification

PMI: CAPM, PMP (bachelor's + 3 years' experience + 35 h training; 4 h, 200-question exam), PgMP. IPMA (ICB: technical, contextual, behavioural): levels D (associate) → C (PM) → B (senior PM) → A (projects director). Certification is "necessary but not sufficient". PMP holders earn a salary premium in all ten top-paying countries cited ([[gpe-slides-0-2026-2027]]).
