# Project management life cycle and process groups

A project's life cycle is the series of phases it passes through from initiation to closure; all projects map onto a generic structure ([[gpe-slides-3-2026-2027]]). Before it comes evaluation (drop the idea or go); after it, collecting the benefits.

## Five process groups (PMBOK)

1. **Initiating** — define the project and obtain authorization to start. Key outputs: [[project-charter]] and stakeholder register ([[stakeholder-analysis]]). Main processes: select PM, review prior analyses, set team, assimilate the business case, identify stakeholders, develop and approve the charter ([[gpe-slides-4-2026-2027]]).
2. **Planning** — establish scope, refine objectives, define the course of action (strategy, intermediate objectives and metrics, resource estimates).
3. **Executing** — coordinate people and resources to carry out the plan (assign resources, monitor work, integrate, motivate).
4. **Monitoring & Controlling** — measure intermediate results, compare with plan, report, solve problems, decide corrective measures and initiate changes.
5. **Closing** — finalize all activities, formally close and obtain customer acceptance.

PMBOK 6th ed. maps **49 processes** onto 5 process groups × 10 [[pmbok-knowledge-areas]].

## Dynamics over time

Effort and people peak during execution; risk and uncertainty decrease over time; the **cost of changes rises** — changes are cheapest on the drawing board, expensive after "the concrete is poured" (BER airport). See [[change-control-and-scope-creep]].

## PM life cycle vs execution life cycle

The PM life cycle (initiating → closing) wraps a product/execution life cycle such as conception → design → development → tests → implementation. MSc-thesis analogy: topic approved → thesis plan → research and writing → defence.
