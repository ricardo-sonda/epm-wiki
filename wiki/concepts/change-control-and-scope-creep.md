# Change control and scope creep

**Scope creep** is uncontrolled enlargement of project scope; together with fuzzy boundaries it makes up "lack of control of scope changes", a listed cause of project failure ([[gpe-slides-1-2026-2027]]; [[project-success-and-failure]]). Changing requirements alone account for 11.8% of failure causes in the cited data.

## Typical symptom (PC1 MC5)

Users keep requesting features after the scope statement was signed off → schedule slips, budget grows, team demotivated → **scope creep caused by poor requirements control** ([[pc1-gpe-2026-2027]]).

## Cost of change over time

The cost of a change rises through the life cycle: cheap during planning ("an eraser and an afternoon"), expensive during execution (demolition, permits, delay). BER approved many design changes during construction near opening dates — the costliest point of the curve ([[gpe-slides-3-2026-2027]]).

## SmartXCharge rule

When LightEnergy requests out-of-scope work, AES assesses schedule, cost, risk and quality implications before either party commits; **no out-of-scope work proceeds without written agreement from both organizations**. The agreed scope remains the baseline; changes are exceptions. The €650k price changes only through a formal contract amendment ([[smartxcharge-business-case-2026-2027]]). Scope-change decisions belong to the [[steering-committee]].
