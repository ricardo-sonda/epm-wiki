# Triple constraint and the five project dimensions

The **triple constraint** ("the fatal triangle") links **resources (cost)**, **specifications (scope/quality)** and **schedule (time)**: changing one affects the others. The course extends it to **five dimensions**: scope, quality, time, cost and risk ([[gpe-slides-1-2026-2027]]).

## Worked examples (slides)

| Dimension | Christmas party | Computerise Lisbon office |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Drinks, dinner, dancing, entertainment for 500 staff + transport | 50 PCs with office software, staff trained |
| Quality | Four-star-hotel equivalent | Top-of-range, high-spec computers |
| Time | 1 Nov → 20 Dec | Complete by 15 Sep |
| Cost | €50,000 | ≤ €100,000 |
| Risk | Medium | Low |

## Exercise pattern (PC1 MC7)

For "organise a training week for 120 engineers": budget ≤ €30,000 → cost; first week of March → time; trainers internationally certified → quality; accommodation, meals, five modules → scope; low probability of cancellations → risk ([[pc1-gpe-2026-2027]]).

## Use in the project

The charter should identify the customer's priority among these dimensions (ASAP completion? no cost variance? full spec compliance?) — see [[project-charter]]. In [[smartxcharge-project]], time (Week 32 ± <1 week) and price (fixed €650k) are tight, so scope control matters: [[change-control-and-scope-creep]].
