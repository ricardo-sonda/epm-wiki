# Stakeholders and stakeholder analysis

Stakeholders are internal or external persons or entities actively involved in the project or whose interests may be affected by its performance or outcome (Pinto 2016, in [[gpe-slides-1-2026-2027]]).

## Typical stakeholders

Promoters, customer, sponsor, users, participants (PM, team, functional managers), suppliers, steering committee, and others (unions, media/influencers, competitors…).

- **Internal:** internal customers, top management, sponsor, team members, functional managers, other PMs.
- **External:** external customers, users, shareholders, suppliers, competitors, regulators ([[gpe-slides-2-2026-2027]]; classification drill in [[pc2-gpe-2026-2027]]).

## Customer ≠ sponsor

- **Customer:** orders/decides what the project is, pays, approves (or not) the result.
- **Sponsor:** provides resources and support, is accountable for enabling success, has the final say on cancelling, promotes the project. Two styles: *governor* (heads the project, approves the plan, provides resources, reviews status) and *"friend in high places"* (removes roadblocks, handles high-level issues and customer/functional relations).
- Apollo: JFK/LBJ = sponsor; US Government/Congress = customer; NASA = performing organization. Bootmaker exercise asks the same question.

## Stakeholder register (Initiating output)

From [[gpe-slides-4-2026-2027]]: identification (name, position, role, contact); assessment (requirements, expectations, influence); classification (internal/external; supportive/neutral/resistant). Questions: WHO, EXPECTATIONS, IMPACTS, POWER, COMMUNICATION needs.

**Seven-column analysis table** (identical in [[pm-template-d1]]): stakeholder · interest in the project · potential impact on the project · what the project requires from them · perceived attitudes/risks · inputs to the communication plan · who manages the relationship.

## Power/interest matrix

| | Low interest | High interest |
|---|---|---|
| **High power** | Keep satisfied | Key person (manage closely) |
| **Low power** | Minimal effort | Keep informed |

PC2 bike-sharing example: city council = key player; residents/shopkeepers = keep informed; transport regulator = keep satisfied; future users = keep informed and involve.

## Managing expectations

Aim for commitment, engagement and buy-in through continuous interaction; build trust; give each stakeholder group its own buy-in plan. Tailor communication to role, interest and influence rather than sending everything to everyone (PC1 quiz item 8); engage resistant stakeholders rather than excluding them (PC2).
