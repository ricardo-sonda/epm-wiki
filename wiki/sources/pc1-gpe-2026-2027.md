# PC1 — Practical Class Week 1

Exercise class (3 Nov 2026) with a ten-statement "How good are your PM skills?" quiz, seven multiple-choice questions, and worked answers to handout exercises 1 and 2 (see [[gpe-questions-and-exercises]]).

## Skills quiz — verdicts and reasons

1. Leaving work breakdown to assignees — **wrong**: the WBS is a core PM responsibility (with team input). → [[work-breakdown-structure]]
2. Picking team members only on technical skills — **wrong**: interpersonal fit matters too.
3. Formal Project Initiation Document/Charter at the start — **right**. → [[project-charter]]
4. Solving schedule slips with the team instead of blaming — **right**.
5. Planning responses to every risk, large or small — **wrong**: prioritise by probability × impact; accept/monitor minor risks. → [[risk-and-uncertainty]]
6. Requirements as the main measure of quality — **right**.
7. Giving deadlines and letting people self-coordinate — **wrong**: coordination/dependencies are the PM's job.
8. Sending all documents to all stakeholders — **wrong**: tailor communication to role, interest, influence. → [[stakeholder-analysis]]
9. On time and on budget as the most important things — **wrong**: that is only project efficiency. → [[project-success-and-failure]]
10. Managing with a detailed schedule and baseline — **right**.

## Multiple choice (answers)

Managing a store is not a project; Flux/Anna 65/35 split = matrix structure; program = related projects managed together for benefits not available individually; choosing between initiatives by strategic fit/ROI = portfolio management; users adding features after scope sign-off = scope creep; sponsor = provides resources and support, promotes, can cancel; training-week dimensions = cost, time, quality, scope, risk.

## Exercises 1–2 (worked)

- Operations-based firms (Autoeuropa, a beverage company) still need PM for new models, process changes, market entry, training. Special Orders Management is project-based; Partners Management is operations-based with occasional projects. → [[what-is-a-project]]
- Customer satisfaction or market success alone are not sufficient success measures; XPTO's rejected app stems from poor communication/requirements (fix: redo at no cost, capture lessons learned); the villa shows different stakeholders judging success differently, and the need to involve the customer throughout. → [[project-success-and-failure]]
